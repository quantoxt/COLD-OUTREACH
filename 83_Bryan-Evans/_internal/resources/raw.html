Bryan Evans

Posted on April 18, 2011April 29, 2015 by Bryan

Born in Pembrokeshire, West Wales in 1964, Bryan Evans has been based in Glasgow, Scotland, since 1988. He specialises in paintings & prints of Glasgow, Edinburgh and other Scottish towns & cities.

He is predominantly a watercolour painter and also produces etchings and mezzotints, as well as a range of reproduction prints. His watercolours and prints have achieved widespread recognition in Scotland, and he was once memorably hailed in the Glasgow Herald as the “New Scottish Impressionist”.

Stripey stockings

Posted on May 30, 2026 by Bryan

If you were to ask me why I paint , I suppose my honest answer would be that I enjoy the process . I like experimenting with and varying the marks I make and the techniques and materials I use to make the work interesting . When I find a satisfying image or subject matter I often examine different ways of dealing with it to see how dramatically a slight variation in how I deal with it affects the end result . This could be reflections on a pavement , leaves on a tree , tiles in a ‘wally’ close , or , as in these paintings ‘stripey stockings .

These are two variations on the same image that I’ve called , ‘Red stripes and a white gown tie ‘ and , ‘Rust stripes and a white sash’ . As is usually the case in my work , the title isn’t really that important other than as a necessary way of differentiating one piece from another .

The first painting is about 30x30cm in size and is painted mainly using acrylic ink , with a little watercolour and gouache . The second is around 36x36cm and is mainly watersoluble Artgraf Tailor shape pigment , with a little traditional watercolour and gouache . This is my first time using the Artgraf blocks , which are a sort of combination between watercolour and , weirdly , tailors’ chalk block . Interesting ….. and I think I’ll have to experiment a little more . The differences between the two paintings are subtle , but , to me at least , satisfying .

Always look on the bright side – a glass half full

Posted on March 1, 2026 by Bryan

‘Memento mori – a glass half full ‘ . The tradition of painting memento mori , has a long history in Western art …. so I thought I’d join in with the cheeryness . Also , there’s something quite satisfying about painting a skull . Don’t worry , the model was made of plastic …….. honest !

Memento mori ‘ translates roughly from the latin to , ‘remember that you have to die ‘ and possibly the most famous and ingenious interpretation of the theme is Hans Holbein’s ‘The ambassadors . If you don’t know it , do a quick google , or better still , if you can , go to see the original in London’s National Gallery ,

Anyhow , to create the composition I’ve combined my fascination with stripes ( and wine I suppose ) to contrast the monochrome material with the splashes of colour of both the pointing hand and the wine . Make what you will of the wasted , spilt ,wine and also that in the glass , yet to be enjoyed . Let’s hope that the glass is half full ….. not half empty ?

I actually began the painting over a year ago , but only completed it very recently . During it’s gestation I’ve unfortunately had more intimations at the subject of the work than has been comfortable and ,depressingly , as I write this , the world isn’t exactly in the healthiest of places is it ? .

But , don’t worry , in the sage words of Eric Idle ,

If life seems jolly rotten,
There’s something you’ve forgotten,
And that’s to laugh and smile and dance and sing.
When you’re feeling in the dumps,
Don’t be silly chumps.
Just purse your lips and whistle.
That’s the thing.
And…

Always look on the bright side of life

‘Memento mori – a glass half full ‘ Acrylic on board 110x 55 cm

Failure !

Posted on January 23, 2026January 26, 2026 by Bryan

I’m a FAILURE ! Over the last couple of days I’ve had a number of posts in my social media feed , congratulating the person who posted it for getting through to the second stage of the Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer Portrait Award 2026 . Now I follow most of these folk and they’re great painters , so this is not a criticism of them . Well done to them all and I’m sure their work will look marvelous ( probably better than my attempt ), if it gets through the next stage and finally gets hung in London’s National Portrait Gallery later in the year .
I thought , though , that I’d use these announcements as an opportunity to present a different narrative . Social media posts celebrate people’s successes , but , let’s face it , there are an awful lot more failures . This is my rejection notice from the same exhibition . This is the notification that most people who entered ( around 1400 or so I believe ) received , though I haven’t yet seen any others shared . And it’s the norm in the art world . Failure is a fact of life in this business .

I’ve been a full-time , professional artist for about 35 years now and have managed to earn a living from my painterly daubs for all those years .So I suppose I’m one of the lucky ones . I’ve even managed to win a few prizes over the years and have had my work hung in some very prestigious exhibitions as well as on the walls of many hundreds , probably thousands of lovely people who have bought my work . I’m very grateful for that and in most respects have had a great time, .Nevertheless , my failures far outnumber my successes . I could probably wallpaper my studio with rejection notices and my bulging studio bins have been testament to the paintings and prints that haven’t worked and I’ve had to discard .

Even many of the successes have been relative failures . Some of what I believed to have been my best , most successful paintings are still in my studio . They may have been exhibited and admired but are now gathering dust in storage . Many of them took days , weeks , hell, sometimes even years to finish and I’m very proud of them . Pride doesn’t pay the bills though so in this regard much of my work has failed. Don’t get me wrong , I’m not really complaining . I chose to paint pictures for a living and no one said it would be easy . I love the challenge of struggling to make the right marks with paint and trying to make something in two dimensions that moves , excites or delights the onlooker .

I never expected to get rich as an artist and that expectation has certainly been realised and I’m hugely grateful that I’ve managed to survive by creating pictures and as a by-product ,often given immense pleasure to many people . Next time you see an artist ( hopefully , sometimes even me ) celebrating their success , bare in mind that that’s just the tiny tip of a very large iceberg , concealing a massive amount of failure and rejection under the surface . I’ve got all that off my chest now , so I’m off to have a drink …. let’s raise a glass to celebrating successes …… and failures .

Glasgow Cathedral

Posted on September 7, 2025 by Bryan

 

This post should probably be titled ‘Glasgow painting delayed because of lack of rain ‘ , but that would be ridiculous wouldn’t it ? Bare with me !

The first painting was commissioned way back in January . The client was married in Glasgow cathedral a number of years a go and wanted a piece of art to commemorate the occasion . Now , people who are familiar with my paintings of Glasgow will know that I favour reflections on wet streets and dusk light . I visited the cathedral precinct to take some photos in January , when dusk was about 4.30 pm but it wasn’t raining . I tried again a few weeks later but , you guessed it , no rain . Now , I’m not actually in Glasgow all that much these days and , for the next few months , every time I was , it was dry . I felt a bit of a fraud making the excuse that I couldn’t get the painting done because it just didn’t rain enough in the west of Scotland , but it was true .

It was actually early June before the heavens opened and I could get my photos . By this time of course , dusk in Scotland is after 10 pm , so , if anyone noticed a bedraggled and damp chap loitering around the cathedral for half an hour when all sensible people should have been tucked up in the dry ….. well it was in the cause of art .

The client finally got to see the painting  and was delighted with it . I positioned a kissing bride and groom in the middle distance to represent him and his wife .

I ended up calling the painting ‘ A kiss by Glasgow cathedral ‘ but I suppose it could have been ‘Waiting for the Glasgow rain ‘ . It’s acrylic on board and about 76 x 50 cm in size .

The other paintings are slightly smaller ones I did using the reference photos
